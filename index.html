About Me &
My Goals

My journey into computer science began with a moment of awe — tasked during an early college course to calculate and generate any calendar month since 1582 using code. When the solution clicked, so did my path. The power of computing became undeniable.

Despite a difficult start at BYU-Idaho, I returned to education in 2019 with renewed purpose. Enrolled in online coursework through the Los Rios Community College District, I earned a 3.95 GPA and completed four Associate's degrees — in Computer Science, Mathematics, and Interdisciplinary Studies — at Folsom Lake College.

My coursework has spanned C++, Python with Pandas, SQL, Assembly language, Data Structures, Artificial Intelligence (chatbots, evolutionary agents), and advanced mathematics including Calculus I–III, Linear Algebra, and Differential Equations. I chose Computer Science because it sits at the convergence of mathematics, pure abstraction, and impactful technology.

I am now pursuing my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science at California State University, Monterey Bay, where I aim to deepen my expertise in software engineering, data analysis, and ultimately quantum computing. I applied for the SMART Scholarship-for-Service Program because I believe contributing to the Department of Defense's mission of technological superiority is one of the highest purposes my skills can serve.

My long-term goal is to work on quantum software development — arguably the most interesting and underdeveloped frontier in computing. The knowledge to be gained from a Master's-level pursuit in this domain, combined with applied DoD research, would put me at the forefront of the next computing revolution.

// Industry Expert Interview Reflection Report

Jonathan Kenney

Principal Electrical Engineer · Raytheon Technologies · Dallas, TX

Jonathan specializes in embedded software and FPGA design for thermal cameras and other defense applications at Raytheon Technologies. After earning a "vanilla" electrical engineering degree from BYU, he took an entry-level role, completed a master's degree aligned to his field, then joined Raytheon — initially on radar, then transferring to the Dallas headquarters to pursue a PhD while working. He joined the beginning of a thermal camera "franchise" and over 7+ years has expanded from FPGA design into embedded software, higher-level software, algorithm improvement, and most recently hardware acceleration.

In his current role he works on accelerating real-time algorithms in FPGAs, ensuring bit-for-bit match with MATLAB and simulation models, validating hardware behavior, and supporting flight testing before deployment. The biggest challenges involve scale and complexity — projects with thousands of lines of code where no single person can know everything, and where fixes can create new bugs because "everything interacts." A newer challenge is meeting demanding performance requirements such as reliably detecting and tracking extremely small targets that appear as a single pixel on screen.

For emerging trends, AI/ML is the most disruptive force — engineers are using it to automate anomaly detection and classification. Ongoing pressures include reducing size/weight/power, increasing sensor resolution and bandwidth, and shortening development cycles via agile "sprints" now expanding beyond software into firmware. Jonathan highlights communication, emotional intelligence (EQ), and strong work ethic as undervalued attributes in new hires. His advice: say "yes" to new opportunities, ask experienced teammates for help when stuck rather than spinning wheels, and build at least one project with real AI/ML exposure. Stay flexible through continuous learning — employer-supported master's work, certificates, seminars — and keep "engineer hobbies" to stay curious and hands-on as technology shifts.

FPGA Design Embedded Software Thermal Camera Systems Real-Time Algorithms AI/ML in Defense Agile Development Hardware Acceleration Size/Weight/Power

Brent Kenney

Digital Communications Analyst · L3Harris Technologies · Utah

Brent has over 13 years at L3Harris Technologies (formerly L3 Communications), having previously supervised an electrical engineering group at Boeing in Anaheim before relocating to Utah in 2012. In 2018 he began a PhD program while his company allowed him to reduce hours at full salary — completing the degree in late 2022 and receiving a promotion thereafter. His work sits on the analysis and system-design side of advanced radios, focused on modems and communication technologies. He partners closely with engineers implementing designs in VHDL on FPGAs while he handles high-level design and analysis in MATLAB. His whole team is vertically integrated in the development of high-end radios.

The biggest challenge Brent navigates is balancing low-level nuts-and-bolts work with high-level design, and deciding when to solve a problem independently versus reaching out to a domain expert. To remain relevant, he emphasizes the need to periodically reinvent what you're known for and avoid falling into ruts. On the industry side, he's watching a shift from FPGA-based modem designs toward more processor-based approaches that could enable smaller, lower-power solutions — alongside "scrappier" new entrants moving cheaper and faster than traditional custom development.

Brent consistently emphasizes communication with different personality types and knowing how to approach people to get the answers you need. His advice to students is direct: bring a real thirst for learning, and whatever task you're given, do it well — that's how you build a reputation for technical excellence that compounds over time.

CST 300

Graduation Writing Assessment for Computing and Design

3 Units · Required

Develops technical writing and communication skills essential for computing professionals. Students produce professional-level written work demonstrating competency in communicating complex technical and social topics related to computing and design to varied audiences.

CST 338

Software Design

4 Units · Required

Provides students with the fundamentals of object-oriented programming in Java. Topics include problem decomposition, abstraction, encapsulation, inheritance, polymorphism, data structures, algorithms, and software design patterns. Project-based course emphasizing team collaboration.

CST 311

Introduction to Computer Networks

4 Units · Required

Survey of telecommunications and data communications technology fundamentals. Covers local area networks (LAN), wide area networks (WAN), Internet and internetworking protocols including TCP/IP, network security and performance, and emerging trends such as voice-over-network and wireless technologies.

CST 334

Operating Systems

4 Units · Required

Covers the use and design of modern operating systems with a focus on Linux. Students learn the Linux command line, shell scripting, and building programs with GNU utilities. Design topics include process management, memory management, file systems, and concurrency with threads and synchronization.

CST 336

Internet Programming

4 Units · Required

Provides students with dynamic web application development skills. Topics include full-stack web development using modern JavaScript (client and server side), HTML5, CSS3, REST APIs, databases, and cloud deployment. Emphasis on practical, project-based learning.

CST 363

Introduction to Database Systems

4 Units · Required

Introduces relational database design and implementation. Covers the entity-relationship model, SQL, normalization, transactions, and database security. Students design and build multi-tiered database-backed applications, complementing prior Python/Pandas and SQL experience.

CST 370

Design and Analysis of Algorithms

4 Units · Required

Fundamental algorithm design paradigms: divide-and-conquer, greedy, dynamic programming, and graph algorithms. Analysis of time and space complexity. Topics include sorting, searching, graph traversal (BFS/DFS), shortest paths, and NP-completeness — critical foundations for DoD computational research.

CST 438

Software Engineering

4 Units · Required

Prepares students for large-scale software development using software engineering principles and techniques. Coverage includes software process, requirements analysis and specification, software design, implementation, testing, and project management. Students work in teams to develop a significant software system.

CST 462S

Race, Gender, Class in the Digital World

3 Units · Required (Upper Div GE)

Examines how social categories of race, gender, and class intersect with digital technologies, computing, and online spaces. Addresses ethical responsibilities of computing professionals in designing equitable systems, satisfying the upper-division GE area 4 requirement.
